## Research Goal
Derive a simple analytic approximation of sedimentation efficiency for a single raindrop as a function of surface relative humidity, surface air temperature, and the raindrop's radius at the lifted condensation level.

![Diagram of Processes Involved in Sedimentation Efficiency](/images/precipitation_efficiency_diagram.jpg)

## Scientific Context
We define precipitation efficiency as the ratio of surface precipitation rate to column-integrated condensation rate. Despite its importance in the climate system, precipitation efficiency is not generally included in representations of the hydrologic cycle. In actuality, precipitation efficiency acts as a control on atmospheric relative humidity, is integral to the formation of cold pools in convective storms, and contributes to setting the intensity and lifetime of tropical cyclones. Following work by Langhans et al. (2016), we think of precipitation efficiency as the product of two components: conversion efficiency, the conditional probability of condensed water vapor forming precipitation, and sedimentation efficiency, the conditional probability of precipitation reaching the surface. We focus our approximation on constraining sedimentation efficiency.

## Approach
We assume a dry adiabat through the subcloud layer and a population of raindrops of uniform radius.
My contribution to this project over the summer was to check and expand upon the tentative derivation started by Dr. Cronin while maintaining the dependence on relative humidity rather than subcloud-layer mass as was used before, and arrive at a final simple approximation for sedimentation efficiency in terms of meaningful variables. I also produced figures in MATLAB to demonstrate the behavior of the approximation under a range of climate regimes.

## Ongoing Work
Beyond the summer, I have continued to work on drafting an introduction to a paper detailing this work, writing an appendix section for this paper showing the relevant guiding equations and how they were derived, and seeking out literature relevant to the theory that would help further simplify our expression.

## Significance
Although past works have explored similar topics to those discussed here and begun on derivations seeking to approximate precipitation efficiency, a fully analytic expression had not yet been demonstrated. This study builds on past work to improve our understanding of precipitation efficiency and to standardize and clarify the terminology used in its description.
