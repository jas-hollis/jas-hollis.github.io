## Research Question
How and where does snow enter the ocean? How does this impact water mass transformation? How is this impact affected by evolving sea ice extent?

## Scientific Context
The Southern Ocean is a major site of surface buoyancy fluxes, facilitating the ventilation of deep ocean properties and the subduction of modified surface waters into the interior. The freshwater fluxes associated with the sea ice formation and melt patterns that characterize the region control the density structure that drives this process. In this study, I focus on the contribution of snow within this sea ice freshwater flux.

Sea ice acts as a regional and seasonal control on snow flux into the ocean, reinforcing the density structure by carrying that snow to melt with it near the sea ice edge. Through this relationship, changes in sea ice extent have a direct path to impact where that freshwater enters the ocean, altering water mass transformation (WMT) rates. As WMT is one of the main drivers of the meridional component of the overturning circulation, such changes could have implications for the strength of the circulation. Past work has included analyses of the contribution of freshwater fluxes to WMT, as compared to heat fluxes, but these studies did not isolate the impacts that snow accumulation has and will have on WMT.

To contribute to closing this research gap, the primary focus of this study is to quantify the impact that snow interception by sea ice has on WMT, and to explore the extent to which snow accumulation on sea ice modulates freshwater fluxes into the surface ocean. The insights gained in the pursuit of these goals will bring about a better understanding of how WMT will change over time and across regions as climate change progresses, and of the broader implications these trends have for the global climate system.

## Approach
Use aspects of ECCO Version 4, release 5 (V4r5) estimate solutions, which span 1992-2024, to study how and where snow enters the ocean, geographically and in density space, and to determine how this impacts WMT rates.
Use aspects of ECCO V4r5 estimate solutions to look for trends regarding how snow on sea ice modulates WMT through its impact on sea ice melt.
